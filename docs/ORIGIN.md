# The Origin of AILex

AILex did not begin as a plan to build a public dictionary or as an experiment in vibe coding. It began with curiosity about a single term.

## The spark: Meat Proxy

The immediate spark was seeing a post on X that defined **Meat Proxy** as a person who forwards AI-generated text, code, or other output without reading, understanding, or validating it, acting only as a relay between the AI system and the intended recipient.

The term was funny, but the larger idea was more interesting: the AI era was already producing its own vocabulary. Some of that language came from research and engineering. Some came from workplaces, product development, online communities, governance, and internet culture. New terms were appearing quickly enough that it felt useful to start collecting them.

The first goal was personal. AILex began as an attempt to catalog AI-related terminology for reference.

## From a list to a dictionary

The earliest version of the idea was simple: ask ChatGPT to help generate a list of AI terms and plain-English definitions.

A static list quickly felt insufficient. The designer instinct behind the project pushed against the idea of leaving the material in a Word document or another long file. If the collection was going to grow, it should be something that could be browsed, searched, organized, and actually used.

That was the first shift from a personal list toward a reference product.

## Curiosity about where the words came from

The project expanded again when the question changed from only **“What does this term mean?”** to **“Where did this term come from?”**

That introduced a second layer of research:

- Was the term coined by someone, or merely popularized by them?
- When can its use be responsibly documented?
- Did the current AI meaning evolve from an older technical or cultural meaning?
- Is the claimed origin actually supported by a primary source?
- How has the term changed as AI technology and culture changed?

Those questions led to AILex's provenance model, source records, history fields, cautious first-known-use policy, and human-reviewed sourcing standard.

## The original name

The project was originally called **Dictionary of the AI Era**. That name described exactly what the project was becoming: a living reference for the language developing around artificial intelligence.

As the product identity matured, **AILex** became the primary name, combining **AI** with **lexicon**. *Dictionary of the AI Era* remains the descriptor and statement of purpose.

AILex is pronounced **/A-I-lex/**, saying the letters A and I followed by “lex.”

## The second experiment: build it with AI

The dictionary came first. The vibe-coding experiment came later.

During development, another thought emerged:

> **“Wouldn't it be cool if the Dictionary of the AI Era was actually built using AI?”**

That question became a second purpose for the project.

AILex would not only document AI-era language. It would also explore what it looks like to build and maintain a serious public reference project through human-directed AI collaboration.

AI began assisting with product exploration, architecture, code, debugging, documentation, research organization, definition drafting, source discovery, QA, and implementation. Human judgment continued to control project direction, editorial inclusion, source evaluation, testing, revisions, merge decisions, and what ultimately gets published.

The resulting development model is summarized as:

> **Vibe coded · Human-directed · AI-assisted · Human-reviewed**

The goal is not to pretend AI built the project independently, nor to hide the amount of AI involvement. The experiment is to see how far human-directed AI collaboration can go while preserving judgment, accountability, transparency, and editorial standards.

## Why open source matters

AILex is intended to remain open source.

That matters for both sides of the project. The dictionary benefits from visible definitions, sources, methodology, corrections, and contributions. The development experiment benefits from being inspectable as well: people can see the architecture, documentation, data, decisions, fixes, and evolution instead of only seeing a polished finished product.

The process has included successful implementations, regressions, browser-specific pronunciation problems, mobile UX corrections, validation failures that prevented bad data from publishing, and documentation that needed to catch up with a rapidly changing product. Those are not separate from the experiment. They are part of what an AI-assisted project looks like in practice.

## What AILex became

What started as a personal catalog of interesting AI terminology has become two connected things:

1. **A living dictionary** of the language developing around artificial intelligence.
2. **An open-source experiment** in building that dictionary transparently through human-directed, AI-assisted development.

The dictionary remains the product. The experiment is part of how the product is made.
