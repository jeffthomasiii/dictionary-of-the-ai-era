Methodology

A transparent editorial process for deciding what belongs in a living dictionary of AI-era language and how each entry should be described.

1. Identify candidate terms

Candidate terms may come from technical documentation, research, industry usage, workplaces, media, online communities, or observed changes in how people talk about AI.

2. Establish real usage

A term should represent documented language in actual use, not merely something an AI model can generate. Emerging slang may require less formal evidence than a technical definition, but AILex should be able to show why the term belongs.

3. Classify the term

Each entry receives one or more categories and a status that helps readers understand what kind of language they are encountering: established, technical, emerging, slang, governance, research, security, contested, or deprecated.

4. Write for clarity

Definitions prioritize plain English, accuracy, and useful distinctions over hype. Each entry includes pronunciation and a natural example sentence so AILex works like a dictionary rather than a technical glossary.

5. Research provenance

Where meaningful, the project will document early usage, who coined or popularized a term, how its meaning developed, and the sources supporting those claims. This provenance layer is a planned editorial milestone.

6. Human review

AI may assist with candidate discovery, research organization, drafting, coding, and editing, but final editorial inclusion and factual judgment remain human-reviewed. AI output by itself is never treated as a source.

7. Revisit as language changes

AILex is a living dictionary. Definitions, statuses, categories, and historical notes may change as usage becomes clearer or the AI landscape changes.

Planned evolution

Future methodology work will include stronger source requirements, individual term histories, related-term relationships, dedicated term pages, audible pronunciation, and a documented editorial review lifecycle.
